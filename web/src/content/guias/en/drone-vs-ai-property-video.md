## When a drone is the right call

A drone records what is there. When you are selling the location as much as the house, nothing replaces it:

- **The surroundings.** What sits around the plot: other villas, a service road, an empty lot, a main road.
- **The approach.** The drive in from the main road, the gradient, the gates.
- **The plot.** Its shape and boundaries, how much of it the house takes up and how much land is left.
- **Long views.** How far away the sea really is, and what you see above the pines.

AI cannot do any of this, and should not try: it works with what is in the photographs. If nobody photographed the access road, the film will not show it; if it invented one, the listing would be lying.

## When AI is the better option

An AI film starts from real photographs of the property. With nothing in the air, the paperwork and the waiting disappear:

- **Timing.** The listing goes live this week, with no room for notice periods, coordination or a windy day.
- **Cost per property.** A film for every listing in the portfolio, not only the flagship: that is where the price per film decides.
- **Restricted airspace.** A villa inside an airport zone, on a development with neighbours or in a protected natural area: for a drone, more paperwork or a refusal; for an AI film, no difference.
- **Weather.** The photos already exist; there is no waiting for the wind to drop.
- **No site visit.** Owners abroad, a let or occupied property: nobody needs to travel.

What it will not do is add anything that is not there: every shot comes from a single photo, the AI computes its depth and the camera moves inside it. ## What flying a drone over a villa in Spain involves

### The rules: one European, one Spanish

[EU Implementing Regulation 2019/947](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019R0947) sets drone rules across the EU, and Spain's [Royal Decree 517/2024](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-11377), in force since 25 June 2024, adds the national layer. Neither distinguishes between hobby and professional flying: the owner's friend with a drone follows the same rules as a production company.

The EU rules sort flights by risk:

- **Open category:** low risk. Drones under 25 kg, no higher than 120 m, always within the pilot's sight. Split into A1, A2 and A3 depending on how close you fly to uninvolved people.
- **Specific category:** anything that does not fit the open category. It needs a declaration to AESA, Spain's aviation safety agency, under a standard scenario, or an operational authorisation.
- **Certified category:** high risk; not relevant to property filming.

The operator must also be registered with AESA (any drone with a camera, toys aside); the pilot needs the training for the category (for A1/A3, [AESA's free online course and exam](https://www.seguridadaerea.gob.es/es/ambitos/drones/formacion-de-pilotos-a-distancia-de-uas-drones/formacion-de-pilotos-uas-drones-en-categoria-rabiertar)); and third-party liability insurance is compulsory in A2, in the specific category and for drones of 20 kg or more.

### Urban areas: five calendar days' notice

The decree treats as urban, among other cases, residential areas with road access, paved public footpaths, drainage and street lighting. A development with all four counts as urban for these purposes. Flying there means:

1. **Notifying the Ministry of the Interior at least five calendar days in advance**, giving the date and time slot of each flight. It is a prior notification, but the security authorities can restrict or ban the flight.
2. **Not flying over buildings or homes, including their recreational areas, patios and similar spaces.** On a villa development, that covers gardens and pools.
3. **Keeping your distance:** at least 5 m from those buildings with a class C1 drone of 250 g or more, and 30 m with a class C2 (5 m in low-speed mode).

The owner of a house can, at the operator's request and before the flight, waive the overflight ban and the distances for their own property. Only their own: their consent does not cover the neighbours' plots.

### Airports: aerodrome zones and controlled airspace

Around every public-use aerodrome, which includes every commercial airport, there is a zone where drones are banned unless the flight is coordinated in advance. It runs 6 km beyond each end of the runway and 5 km either side of its centreline, from the ground up to 45 m; between 45 m and 900 m, it widens to 10 km and 7.5 km.

If the airport also has controlled airspace (a CTR), then inside it and outside the aerodrome zone you may fly without coordination only within sight and up to 60 m. Any other flight must be coordinated with the air traffic services provider: ENAIRE, Spain's air navigation service provider, through its ENAIRE Planea app, or another provider depending on the airport.

To coordinate, the operator has to justify why the flight must happen at that exact spot. The airport or the provider has up to a month to respond, and if there is no agreement within that time the request is deemed refused. If it goes ahead, confirmation comes at least 72 hours before the flight.

In Mallorca, Palma airport has a CTR that starts at ground level ([ENAIRE AIP](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/contenido_AIP/AD/AD2/LEPA_LESJ/LE_AD_2_LEPA_LESJ_es.pdf), 3 September 2026 edition). So do [Málaga-Costa del Sol](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/contenido_AIP/AD/AD2/LEMG/LE_AD_2_LEMG_en.pdf), [Alicante-Elche](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/contenido_AIP/AD/AD2/LEAL/LE_AD_2_LEAL_en.pdf), [Gran Canaria](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/contenido_AIP/AD/AD2/GCLP/LE_AD_2_GCLP_en.pdf) and [Tenerife South](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/contenido_AIP/AD/AD2/GCTS/LE_AD_2_GCTS_en.pdf), among others.

### Protected areas and aerial photography

In protected natural areas, AESA says prior coordination with the body that manages them is needed. In zones restricted for aerial photography, taking images from the air requires a prior request to the Ministry of Defence's cartographic and photographic centre (CECAF).

All of these zones are on one official map, [ENAIRE Drones](https://drones.enaire.es/), which itself warns that the information can change at any time. Check the exact plot before asking anyone for a quote.

### Weather

We have found no rule that sets a maximum wind speed; each manufacturer sets its own limit. DJI, for instance, rates its [Mavic 4 Pro](https://www.dji.com/mavic-4-pro/specs) for winds of up to 12 m/s, around 43 km/h, and its spec sheet lists no water-resistance rating. A windy or grey day means postponing the shoot, and if the new date falls outside the dates and time slots notified to the Interior Ministry, it has to be notified again with the same notice.

## Five questions to decide

1. **Do buyers need to see the surroundings, the access or the plot?** Drone.
2. **What does ENAIRE Drones show for the plot?** Airport zone, CTR, protected area or urban development: a drone is possible, with lead times and paperwork. If none applies, it is the simple option.
3. **When does the listing go live?** If it is this week, AI.
4. **How many properties?** For one, what you need to show decides; across a portfolio, the cost per film matters.
5. **Do you have good, recent photos?** Without them there is no AI film; with them, it takes 72 hours.

You do not have to choose once and for all: a drone for the property where the setting is the selling point, AI films for the rest of the portfolio. If aerial photos from an earlier shoot already exist, they work as a starting point too.
